# 2026-09-02 — Lista: a fourth view that browses the corpus instead of playing it

## Decision: add a reverse-chronological list view that ignores the timeline cursor, and hide the timeline bar and dock feed while it is open

## Context

All three existing views answer "what changed *when*" — they are gated on the
timeline cursor, and the dock feed shows the cursor's trailing slice. None of
them answers the plainer question "what are the most recent laws, and can I
read down the list?". With 2500 events the radar's outer rings are dense
(a trade-off accepted in the 2026-08-16 record), so a plain list is also the
cheapest legibility fix available.

## Alternatives considered

- **Cursor-gate the list like everything else.** Consistent, but the cursor
  defaults to the *start* of coverage, so the list would open empty and the
  view would only work after scrubbing to the end — the opposite of "newest
  first".
- **Pagination.** Explicitly ruled out by the request, and page boundaries
  fight the month/year dividers that give the list its structure.
- **Render all 2500 rows at once.** ~2500 rows × 4 elements is survivable but
  wasteful on every filter change, and the first paint is the one that matters.
- **Reuse the dock feed, widened.** It is incremental, append-only and pinned
  to the bottom — the wrong shape for newest-first browsing, and it would still
  be cursor-gated.
- **IntersectionObserver sentinel for the chunking** (written first). Replaced
  by a scroll listener plus a synchronous top-up: one mechanism instead of two,
  no callback that a throttled tab can suspend, and it is verifiable by
  dispatching a scroll event rather than by needing a real viewport.

## Reasoning

The list is the "browse everything" view, so the timeline has nothing to say in
it: hiding the bar removes a control that would silently do nothing, and gives
the list its height. The dock feed is the cursor-following slice of the same
data, so next to the list it would read as a broken near-duplicate — hidden
too. Everything else stays shared through the same store: legend filters, the
dimension switcher (which tints the row swatches) and the search box all still
apply, and the view's own chips write to the same `filters.domains` /
`filters.jurisdictions` facets, so chips and legend can never disagree.

Chunking at 200 rows keeps the first paint cheap; the sync top-up covers the
case where one chunk does not fill a tall window, and a rebuild (filter or
query change) restarts from the top.

Verified against the real 2500-event dataset: 200 rows on first paint growing
to 2500 by scroll, 27 year dividers and 299 month dividers with no duplicate or
missing divider across chunk seams, rows descending within every month.

## Trade-offs accepted

- **Two places to filter by domain** (dock legend, list chips). They share
  state, so they cannot diverge, but the dock legend is redundant here.
- **A view that opts out of the app's main control.** The timeline bar
  disappearing on view switch is a visible discontinuity; it is preferable to
  a visible control that does nothing.
- **Row height is not virtualized.** At 2500 rows the fully-scrolled DOM is
  ~2800 elements. Fine at this dataset size; a much larger corpus would want
  windowing rather than a bigger `CHUNK`.
